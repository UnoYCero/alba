One instrument. Three missions.

The Alba Space Star Tracker is a 3-in-1 optical instrument for CubeSats. A single sensor, with a telescopic baffle that deploys in orbit, finds its orientation from the stars alone, detects space debris and guides docking maneuvers.

A single camera does three jobs.

In a traditional mission, attitude determination, watching for space debris and docking with another vehicle require different cameras and equipment: more cost, more mass and more power. Our instrument runs all three algorithms in parallel, on a flight computer with a real-time operating system.

How it knows where it's pointing.

It solves on its own what is called the «lost in space» problem: knowing nothing beforehand, it photographs the sky, recognizes the stars and calculates its orientation. Tap a stage or let it run.

Debris hidden among the stars.

Between two consecutive photos the stars do not move, but debris and satellites do. The instrument subtracts one photo from the other, cleans up what is left over and keeps what moves: its position, heading and speed.


        

        
      

    


    
    

      

        

          
A zoom made of structure.

          
The baffle does more than block light. Extending it moves the optics and changes the focal length: the same sensor goes from watching more than 20 degrees of sky to a close view of the vehicle it is about to dock with.

A baffle that deploys on its own in orbit.

          
A fixed baffle takes up valuable volume at launch. Ours travels stowed, less than 50 mm long, and extends once it reaches orbit.

          
    
            
  • Up to half the volume
    It blocks stray light in orbit without taking space from the CubeSat payload: 0.13 dm³ stowed and 0.28 dm³ deployed.
    • 
            
  • Release with a backup
    A dual circuit cuts a wire by heat and spring-loaded guides push each segment into place.
    • 
            
  • Built for vacuum
    Teflon bushings keep the metal parts from cold-welding in vacuum, so it works after months in orbit.
